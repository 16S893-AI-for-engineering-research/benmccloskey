Project outline

An agentic, verifiable framework for MEDEVAC dispatch modeling

Drawn from my week-1 one-page proposal draft (see repo). Not yet advisor-signed — sign-off is due 22 September.

Research question

Can an AI agent reliably verify that MEDEVAC dispatch optimization formulations — mixed-integer programs (MIPs) and Markov decision processes (MDPs) — satisfy explicit mathematical and operational invariants, and produce behavior consistent with their intended formulation?

Open item: "reliably" still needs a precise definition — likely a catch rate on a seeded-bug suite plus a false-alarm rate. Not yet defined.

Why this is live research, not a course toy

As part of my PhD, I'm developing new optimization formulations for dispatching MEDEVAC assets (helicopters and ground ambulances). My committee has pointed me toward two directions: MIP and MDP models. This course project builds a structured environment for studying whether an agent can help construct and verify those formulations — checking that constraints are feasible, internally consistent, and aligned with intended operational behavior — and eventually add an explainability (XAI) layer for sensitivity and counterfactual analysis grounded in operations research fundamentals.

Most existing MEDEVAC dispatch literature leans on sequential decision-making (MDPs); MIP-based formulations are comparatively under-explored. This framework doubles as a verification environment for the course and as groundwork for my dissertation's final dispatch formulation, which aims to incorporate casualty physiological state (e.g. blood pressure, other deterioration measures) directly into resource-allocation decisions.

Where delegation stops

Delegation stops when the agent reports a violation in a formulation or invariant check — I decide whether the model, the invariant, or the scenario itself is wrong. The agent does not get to silently "fix" the model. Out of scope for this term: replacing the optimizer itself, and new physiology modeling (that's downstream dissertation work, not this course project).

Term-success metric is still a placeholder in the draft (e.g. "catch k of n seeded MIP bugs") — to be pinned down before the 22 September sign-off.
